What this tool can and can't do

Nebula Vision turns a phone camera into a rough inspection instrument. It is honest about its limits — read this before trusting a number.

Reliable

  • Distances and diameters, once a reference object of known size is placed in the same plane as the part and the camera is roughly perpendicular to that plane. Typical error: 1–3% at arm's length with a good reference.
  • Gross shape classification (round vs. hex vs. rectangular) and approximate hole positions.
  • Nominal fastener size matching, when the shaft or hex head is clearly visible and calibrated.

Not reliable — and why

  • Thread pitch cannot be measured from a normal photo; threads are sub-millimetre and need a macro lens or the app's coarse pitch-from-length inference (an estimate, never a measurement).
  • Any dimension out of the calibration plane (depth, features on a tilted face) is distorted by perspective. The app warns when a surface looks tilted but cannot fully correct it without a second angle.
  • Surface finish, hardness, and sub-millimetre defects (hairline cracks, small burrs) are usually below phone-camera resolution.
  • Lens distortion (barrel/pincushion) is not corrected per-device; error grows near the frame edges — keep the part centred.

How calibration works

You mark two points on a reference of known length (a ruler, a coin, a credit card, or a bolt you already know). The pixel distance between them, divided into the known length, gives a pixels-per-millimetre scale for that photo only — move the camera and you must recalibrate, unless you capture a new view with its own reference.

Multi-view

Add more than one photo of the same part and the app averages repeated measurements, reporting the spread (±) as an uncertainty rather than a single confident number.
